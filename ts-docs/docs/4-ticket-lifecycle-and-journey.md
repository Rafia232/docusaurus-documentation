# 4. Ticket Lifecycle and Journey

The ticket lifecycle explains how a support request moves through the MoH Helpdesk from creation to closure.

A ticket may be created from the **Public Portal**, **Email**, or the internal **Create Ticket** form. After creation, the ticket is reviewed, assigned, updated, tracked, and finally closed by the support team.

---

## 4.1 Ticket Lifecycle Overview

The usual ticket journey includes the following steps:

| Step | Stage                | Description                                                                             |
| ---- | -------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1    | **Setup**            | Admin configures users, roles, teams, categories, portal access, email, and automation. |
| 2    | **Ticket Creation**  | A ticket is created from the public portal, email, or internal form.                    |
| 3    | **Routing**          | The ticket is routed based on category and team mapping.                                |
| 4    | **Assignment**       | The ticket is assigned to a team, expert, or responsible person.                        |
| 5    | **Work in Progress** | The support team reviews the issue and starts working on it.                            |
| 6    | **Communication**    | Facility users and support staff communicate through ticket replies.                    |
| 7    | **Monitoring**       | The ticket is tracked by status, priority, due date, and activity timeline.             |
| 8    | **Closure**          | The ticket is closed after the issue is resolved or completed.                          |

---

## 4.2 Before a Ticket Starts

Before tickets can be handled properly, administrators should complete the basic setup.

This includes creating users, assigning roles, setting permissions, enabling the public portal, creating Service Desk teams, creating categories, mapping categories with teams, and configuring email notifications.

A correct setup helps tickets reach the right team quickly.

---

## 4.3 Ticket Creation

Tickets can be created in three ways:

| Channel           | Description                                                               |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Public Portal** | Facility users create and track their own support tickets.                |
| **Email**         | Users send support requests through the configured email address.         |
| **Internal Form** | Admins, Agents, or Experts create tickets directly from the Service Desk. |

Facility users should provide a clear title, detailed description, correct category, contact information, and attachments if needed.

---

## 4.4 Ticket Routing and Assignment

After a ticket is created, it is routed to the correct support team based on the selected category.

For example, a **Network Issue** can be routed to the Network Support Team, while a **Software Issue** can be routed to the Software Support Team.

The ticket can then be assigned to a specific expert or responsible person.

---

## 4.5 Ticket Review

Service Desk users can review tickets from the ticket dashboard or ticket list.


From here, users can check the ticket number, title, created date, source, assignee, priority, status, and available actions.

They can also search, filter, export, open, edit, assign, or update tickets based on their permissions.

---

## 4.6 Ticket Status and Priority

Ticket status shows the current progress of the request.

| Status          | Meaning                                                                             |
| --------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Open**        | The ticket has been created but work has not started or finished yet.               |
| **In Progress** | The support team is working on the issue.                                           |
| **Overdue**     | The ticket has exceeded the configured time threshold without progress or activity. |
| **Closed**      | The issue has been resolved or completed.                                           |

Ticket priority shows how urgent the issue is.

Common priority levels may include **Low**, **Medium**, **High**, and **Critical**.

---

## 4.7 Communication and Updates

The Ticket Details page shows the full information of a ticket.


It may include the ticket summary, source, priority, status, assigned team, assigned expert, facility user information, attachments, conversation history, and activity timeline.

Facility users and support staff can communicate through ticket replies.


Facility users can also attach files when replying to a ticket.


---

## 4.8 Reassignment

A ticket can be reassigned when another expert or team is better suited to handle the issue.

Reassignment is useful when the ticket was assigned incorrectly, another team needs to take action, or workload needs to be adjusted.

The full ticket history remains available after reassignment.

---

## 4.9 Overdue Tickets

A ticket is marked as **Overdue** when it exceeds the configured time threshold without progress or activity.

Overdue tracking helps the support team identify delayed tickets and take action quickly.

Overdue tickets may appear in dashboard summary cards and may also trigger email alerts if automation is configured.

---

## 4.10 Email Notifications

The system may send email notifications when important ticket actions happen.

Common notifications include:

| Notification                | When It Is Sent                 |
| --------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| **New Ticket Alert**        | When a new ticket is created.   |
| **Ticket Assignment Alert** | When a ticket is assigned.      |
| **New Message Alert**       | When a new reply is added.      |
| **Internal Note Alert**     | When an internal note is added. |
| **Overdue Ticket Alert**    | When a ticket becomes overdue.  |
| **Ticket Close Alert**      | When a ticket is closed.        |

Email notifications help users stay updated without checking the system manually every time.

---

## 4.11 Ticket Closure

A ticket is closed when the issue is resolved or completed.

Before closing a ticket, the support team should review the issue, confirm the required action is complete, update the user if needed, and then change the ticket status to **Closed**.

After closure, the ticket remains available for future review and record keeping.

---

## 4.12 Simple Ticket Journey Example

A common ticket journey may look like this:

1. Admin configures users, teams, categories, and email notifications.
2. A facility user logs in to the public portal.
3. The facility user opens a new ticket.
4. The ticket appears in the Service Desk.
5. The ticket is routed to the correct team.
6. The ticket is assigned to an expert.
7. The expert reviews the issue and starts working.
8. The facility user and support team communicate through replies.
9. The ticket is updated until the issue is resolved.
10. The ticket is closed.
11. A closure notification may be sent to the facility user.

---

## 4.13 Summary

The ticket lifecycle helps every support request move in a clear and organized way.

A good ticket journey depends on proper admin setup, correct category selection, clear assignment, timely communication, regular monitoring, and proper closure.
